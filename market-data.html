What water rights actually sell for

Published transaction prices and wholesale benchmarks for water in the western United States, drawn from WestWater Research reporting and public studies of wholesale water prices.

There is no single national price for water rights. A permanent right on the Colorado Front Range can trade for tens of thousands of dollars per acre-foot, while a one-year agricultural transfer in California can change hands for a few hundred dollars per acre-foot. The gap is the point: seniority, location, and whether the deal is a permanent sale or a seasonal lease move the price more than any statewide average. One acre-foot equals 326,000 gallons. The figures below keep sale, lease, and wholesale prices in separate rows so they are never read as one market.

How to read these numbers

Included: permanent-right prices reported from WestWater Research's annual report, individual California transfers and the Nasdaq Veles California Water Index as reported in WestWater's weekly California reports, and wholesale-price comparisons from the UCLA / Natural Resources Defense Council study of the Lower Colorado River Basin. Excluded: retail household water bills, which measure delivery and treatment costs rather than the price of the right itself, and any asking price that could not be tied to a reported transaction. Permanent sales, seasonal leases, and wholesale delivery prices are different products; the table labels each row so a lease price is never mistaken for a sale price. The 1987–2008 median series is dated and shown only to illustrate the long-standing gap between sale and lease prices and between agricultural and urban buyers.
